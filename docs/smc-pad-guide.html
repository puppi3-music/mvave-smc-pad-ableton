Using your SMC-PAD with Live

The controller has three presets: one for launching clips, one for programming drum patterns, and one for playing a synth with the pads as a keyboard. Preset 1 also has a mixer one button press away.

First time? Do the one-off Setup at the bottom of this page, then follow Example sessions from the top. They take you from an empty Live set to a recorded jam.

Example sessions

Four walkthroughs that build on each other: make a beat, play over it, add a bassline, then perform and record the whole thing. Each one works on its own too. You switch presets by holding Shift and pressing pad 1, 2 or 3 (the bottom row, from the left), and nothing in Live needs changing when you do.

Session 1

Program a beat from scratch

Preset 2About 5 minutesNeeds a Drum Rack kit
  1. In Live's browser, open Drums and drag any Drum Rack kit onto an empty MIDI track.
  2. In Session view, double-click an empty clip slot on that track. Live makes a one-bar clip and selects it. One bar is 16 steps, one per pad.
  3. Switch the controller to preset 2. dark pads: the clip is empty. Steps read left to right, top to bottom, like text, so the left-hand column is the four beats of the bar (steps 1, 5, 9 and 13).
  4. Kick. Hold Record and check the bottom-left pad is white: that's C1, the kick in almost every kit. If it isn't white, press it. Let go of Record, then press the four pads of the left-hand column. four red-pink pads down the left side, a four-on-the-floor kick.
  5. Press Play to launch the clip. an amber light stepping across the pads. That's the playhead, and it turns white as it passes a step that's on.
  6. Snare or clap. Hold Record and press the third pad on the bottom row (D1, snare) or the fourth (D#1, clap). Let go and press steps 5 and 13: the second and fourth pads down the left column. That's the backbeat, on beats 2 and 4. two green pads. The kick's steps are gone from view because the pads show one drum at a time; it's still playing.
  7. Hi-hats. Hold Record and press the third pad on the second row up (F#1, closed hat). Let go and press the whole third column: steps 3, 7, 11 and 15, the off-beats. a light-blue column. You now have a full house/pop groove.
  8. See it all at once. Hold Record, press the kick again, let go, then press and release Stop. four rows of four steps: kick on the bottom row, then C#1, snare and clap above it, the same order as Live's piano roll. < > move along the bar four steps at a time. Press Stop again to go back.
  9. Shape the sound. The knobs on knob bank 1 turn the Drum Rack's macros. To tweak just the kick (its decay, say), hold Stop and press >: the knobs move to the drum you last picked. Hold Stop and press < to go back to the rack.

Mistake? Cmd+Z in Live undoes the last step. The pattern lives in the clip, so saving the set saves it.
Want more bars? Hold Record and press > to add a bar, or < to remove one. The pads follow the playhead, so each bar shows up as it plays. To edit one ahead of time, press >.

Session 2

Finger-drum a fill over the beat

Preset 2 · PAD BANKAbout 2 minutes
  1. Arm the drum track: click its arm button in Live, or use the mixer (session 4).
  2. With the beat from session 1 playing, press PAD BANK. the pads go dark: they're now the Drum Rack's 16 pads, laid out as Live shows them, with C1 (usually the kick) bottom-left. They don't light, but they're velocity-sensitive: hit harder for louder.
  3. Play along. Hit a tom run at the end of the bar, or hold a hat pad with the controller's note repeat on for a roll (the repeats come out softer than a hit).
  4. Press PAD BANK again to go back to the steps.

Keep what you played. Press Live's Capture MIDI button (next to the record buttons in the control bar) just after playing something you liked. Live adds it to the clip, even though you weren't recording.

Session 3

Add a bassline or chords, and record it

Preset 3Preset 1 to recordAbout 10 minutes
  1. Put a synth on a new MIDI track, next to the drums. Any instrument works with the knobs: Live's own, a rack or a plugin.
  2. Arm the synth track. The keyboard always plays the armed track (or one set to Monitor In).
  3. Switch to preset 3 and press PAD BANK to pick a key. For A minor: press A (second row from the top, second pad), then Scale ‹ or Scale › until Live's status bar names Minor. Live's own Scale setting changes to match. Press PAD BANK to play. blue pads on the root notes (A), purple on the fifths (E), white on every other note in the scale. Every pad is in key, so nothing you hit sounds wrong.
  4. For a bassline, press PAD BANK, then Oct − once or twice (it shows turquoise, then light blue, then bright blue: the octave it will take you to next), and PAD BANK again. Start on the bottom-left root and move between it and the purple fifth.
  5. For chords, use this shape: any pad in the left two columns, the pad two to its right, and the pad diagonally up and to the right of where you started. On a 7-note scale that's always a triad in key. From the bottom-left root it's the root chord, with the purple fifth on top. from C in C major: C3, E3, G3.
  6. Shape the sound. Click the synth so it has Live's blue hand. The knobs now control its main parameters, the same ones Push shows, with no mapping. Knob bank 2 has the next eight. See The knobs.
  7. Record it into a clip: a. Switch to preset 1 and press the empty pad in the synth's column. It goes orange: an empty 1-bar clip. Press it again to play it (green). b. Click Live's Session Record button (the circle in the control bar), switch to preset 3 and play your part. It goes into the looping clip, layer on layer. c. Click Session Record again to stop recording. The clip keeps looping.

Skip the recording dance. Just jam on preset 3, and when you've played something you like, press Live's Capture MIDI button. Live makes a clip of it, already looped and in time.
Want notes outside the scale? On PAD BANK, press the blue key again for the chromatic layout.

Session 4

Jam the track and record it as an arrangement

Preset 1 · PAD BANKAs long as you like

Before you start, lay your clips out in Session view so each row is a section of the song: say row 1 is the intro (hats only), row 2 the verse (drums and bass), row 3 the chorus (everything). Duplicate a clip into the row below and change it to make variations.

  1. Switch to preset 1. Live draws a coloured outline around 4 tracks × 4 rows, and the pads mirror it. orange pads where there are clips, dark where the slots are empty. If your tracks aren't inside the outline, switch to knob bank 2 and turn its bottom two knobs to move it: bottom-left moves across tracks, bottom-right moves up and down rows.
  2. Press Record. Live starts recording into the Arrangement and keeps a copy of everything you launch from here on. Skip this if you just want to jam.
  3. Intro. Press the pads in the top row to start them. each pad blue, then green on the next bar, when the clip starts.
  4. Next section. Press the pads in the next row down. They take over from the clips above at the start of the next bar, so press them anywhere in the bar before. To drop a part out, press PAD BANK and that track's pad on the mixer's bottom row: that stops the track. (An empty slot still stops an audio track, but on a MIDI track it makes a new clip.)
  5. A breakdown. Press PAD BANK for the mixer. Press the drums' pad in the top row to mute them (it goes dark), play a bar or two, then press it again to bring them back on the one. The second row solos a track and the bottom row stops a track's clip. Press PAD BANK to get back to your clips.
  6. A filter sweep. Select a track with < >. The knobs on knob bank 1 turn the macros of that track's selected device, usually its first. With an Audio Effect Rack that has Auto Filter's frequency on macro 1, the top-left knob is your sweep.
  7. Press Stop to finish (Live stops when you let go). Press Tab in Live to see your performance laid out in Arrangement view, ready to edit.

Want the mutes and knob moves recorded too? Turn on Live's Automation Arm button (next to the record buttons in the control bar) before you press Record. Solo isn't recorded; it's a listening aid in Live.
No scene button. The controller has no button to launch a whole row. Press each pad in the row within the same bar and they all start together.

The three presets

Switching modes

Hold Shift and press pad 1, 2 or 3 (the bottom row, from the left) for preset 1, 2 or 3. The scripts stay loaded in Live the whole time, so you don't change anything in Live when you switch.

When Live opens, it puts the controller on preset 1 (the clip launcher) with PAD BANK off, whatever it was left on. That needs MVave_SMC_KNOBS with its output set; see Setup.

Then the pads say hello: a rainbow runs diagonally across them from pad 1 (bottom-left) to the top-right corner for about 3 seconds, then pad 1 flashes white for about 2 seconds to show you're on preset 1. After that your clips' colours come back. The pads work as normal the whole time. Shift doesn't light up: there's no known way for the computer to light it. To turn the intro off, set INTRO_ENABLED = False in MVave_SMC_PAD/MIDI_Map.py. INTRO_COLOUR_TICKS in the same file sets how fast the colours move: 1 (the fastest that still shows every colour) or 2 for half speed.

Within a preset, PAD BANK flips the pads between two layouts. The second layout is the mixer on preset 1, drum pads on preset 2 and the key and scale settings on preset 3.

Preset 1 Clip launcher Pads fire clips in Session view and light up with each clip's state. Preset 2 Step sequencer Pads become 16 steps of a drum pattern in the selected MIDI clip. PAD BANK flips to drum pads. Preset 3 Keyboard Play a synth in any key and scale, Launchpad-style, with the knobs on the instrument's main controls.
Preset 1

Clip launcher

Use this in Session view. Live draws a coloured outline around a block of 4 tracks × 4 scenes. The 16 pads are that block, in the same positions on screen: the top-left pad is the top-left slot.

What the colours mean

  • Playing
  • Clip loaded, stopped
  • Waiting to start
  • Recording
  • Waiting to record
  • Empty slot

A clip waits for the next bar (Live's launch quantisation) before it starts, so pads go blue first, then green.

Preset 1 · PAD BANK

Mixer

Press PAD BANK to turn the pads into a mixer for the same 4 tracks the coloured outline covers. Each column is one track, and each row is one switch. Press PAD BANK again to get your clips back.

The top row works like the track activator buttons in Live's mixer: lit means you hear the track. A dark pad in that row is a muted track.

Preset 2

Step sequencer

This mode edits the selected MIDI clip directly. Nothing is stored on the controller, so saving the Live set saves the pattern and Cmd+Z undoes a step.

It needs the MVave_SMC_STEPSEQ row from Setup.

Making a beat

  1. Put a Drum Rack on a MIDI track and create an empty clip on it: double-click an empty slot, or press an empty pad in its column on preset 1 (a 1-bar clip).
  2. Select the clip so its notes show in Live's clip view.
  3. Switch the controller to preset 2 with Shift + its pad.
  4. Hold Record: the pads show the drums you can pick, laid out like the Drum Rack (bottom-left is C1, usually the kick). The drum you're editing is white. Press a pad to choose another.
  5. Let go, then press pads to switch steps on and off. Each pad is one 16th note, reading left to right and top to bottom.

What the colours mean

  • Step is on, in the drum's colour (see below)
  • Playhead
  • Playhead on a step that's on
  • Step is off, or past the end of the loop

While Live plays, the pads follow the playhead, so 32- and 64-step clips need no paging. If you page away, the view jumps back to the playhead when the loop comes round.

Live's status bar, at the bottom of the window, names each step you change (Kick (C1) step 5/16 on) and which steps the pads are showing (Steps 17-32 of 64). The < > buttons light when there's another page that way.

Which drum am I on?

Hold Record and the pads switch from steps to drums, until you let go:

  • The drum you're editing now
  • Every other drum, in its own colour, the same as its steps (see Drum colours)
  • An empty Drum Rack pad

While the clip plays, each drum's pad flashes white every time the pattern plays it, so you can watch the kit play along and see which drums are in the pattern. The white pad (the drum you're editing) flashes its own colour instead. Only the clip's playback flashes; hits on the PAD BANK drum pads don't.

Live's status bar, at the bottom of the window, also names the drum, for example Lane C1 (36) Kick, and the range the pads can pick. If no pad is white, the drum you're editing is in another set of 16: hold Record and Stop and press < > to find it.

Drum colours

Steps light in the colour of the drum they play, in both views, so you can tell what you're editing at a glance:

  • Kick
  • Snare, clap, rim
  • Hi-hats
  • Cymbals: crash, ride, china, splash
  • Toms
  • Anything else

The script goes by the Drum Rack pad's name (usually the sample's), so a pad called Kick 808 is a kick wherever it sits. Pads with no sound, or a track with no Drum Rack, go by the standard General MIDI drum notes instead: C1 kick, D1 snare, F#1 hat and so on. The same colours show on the drums while you hold Record.

Step velocity comes from Shift + pad 9–12, not from how hard you hit.

The pads that light up can't sense how hard you hit them, so steps are written at the velocity you pick with Shift + pad 9–12: 40, 70, 100 or 127. When Live starts, steps are at full velocity (127), whatever the controller was left on, until you pick one. Picking one needs MVave_SMC_KNOBS with its output set (see Setup). For softer and louder steps in one pattern, change the velocity between steps, or edit them with the mouse in Live's clip view.

Playing a synth over the pattern

Start the pattern playing, then switch to preset 3, pick your synth track with < >, arm it and play. The pattern keeps playing, and the sequencer remembers its clip while you're away: switch back to preset 2 and the pads show your pattern, not the synth track. From your next click in Live it follows the selection again.

Playing over the pattern

Press PAD BANK and the pads play the Drum Rack directly, velocity-sensitive and unlit, so you can play fills over the pattern. Playing a pad doesn't change which drum the steps edit; use Record + pad for that. The buttons and knobs stay on the sequencer's controls, so Play, Stop and the macros still work. Press PAD BANK again to go back to editing steps.

See Drum pads. If the step pads are dark or out of date when you come back, start playback or press < then > to redraw them.

Preset 3

Keyboard

Like a Launchpad's Note mode: the pads play only the notes of the key and scale you choose, starting from the root at bottom-left. Each row starts three notes of the scale above the row below, so the last pad of a row and the first pad of the next play the same note. Roots light blue, fifths light purple so chord shapes stand out, and the other notes white. PAD BANK flips to the key and scale settings, and the buttons work as in the clip launcher, so the arrows choose the track you play.

The pads play at full velocity however hard you hit them, because pads that can light up always send full velocity. It needs MVave_SMC_PAD loaded; see Setup.

Playing

  1. Put a synth (a VST or one of Live's instruments) on a MIDI track.
  2. Arm the track, or set its Monitor to In. Leave the track's input on All Ins and All Channels. The keyboard plays on MIDI channel 2, so if your synth only listens to channel 1, set it to omni or channel 2.
  3. Switch the controller to preset 3 with Shift + its pad.
  4. Play. Bottom-left is the root note. In C major, the default, the pads run from C3 (middle C) to A4.

Point at a pad on the key and scale drawing (or tap it) to see the notes the keyboard would play after pressing it.

Changing key, scale and octave

Press PAD BANK. The bottom three rows choose the key: press a note and it lights blue. Press the blue key again to switch layout (below); in the chromatic layout it lights white-blue instead. The top row moves the keyboard down or up an octave and steps back or forward through the scales. Live's status bar, at the bottom of the window, shows the new key, scale, layout and the notes the pads now cover. Press PAD BANK again to play.

  • Each octave pad lights in the colour of the octave it would take you to, on a cold-to-hot scale: purple at the lowest octave, then blue, light blue, turquoise, yellow (octave 3, where it starts, C3 at bottom-left), orange, red-pink, pink, light pink and white at the highest. At the start, Oct − is turquoise and Oct + orange. A pad goes dark when you can't go further that way.

The key and scale are the same ones as Live's Scale setting in the control bar. Change them on the controller and Live follows; change them in Live and the pads follow.

The scales, in order: Major, Minor, Dorian, Mixolydian, Lydian, Phrygian, Locrian, Harmonic Minor, Melodic Minor, Major Pentatonic, Minor Pentatonic and Minor Blues. The list goes round, and when you get back to Major the scale pad you pressed flickers white three times, so you know you're at the start.

Chromatic layout

Like a Launchpad's chromatic mode: every pad is one semitone up from the last, starting from the key at bottom-left, so the 16 pads cover an octave and a bit. The scale stays lit in the same colours, and the notes outside it are dark but still play, for passing notes and chromatic runs.

It works with any key and scale. To switch, press PAD BANK and press the blue key: it turns white-blue while you're in chromatic. Press it again to go back to playing in key.

Note repeat

The controller's note repeat follows the keyboard: hold a pad and it repeats the note that pad plays, in the current key, scale, octave and layout. The repeats come out quieter than a normal hit, because the controller sends them at a low velocity of its own. Note repeat needs MVave_SMC_KNOBS loaded, and Track off for SINCO SMC-PAD-Master; see Setup.

The knobs

On this preset the knobs control the device that has Live's blue hand: click a device (or its track) and the knobs follow. They're the parameters Ableton picked for its own controllers, so Live's instruments and effects work with no mapping.

DeviceKnob bank 1Knob bank 2
Live's instruments and effectsLive's first bank of 8 (the main controls)Live's second bank
RacksMacros 1–8Macros 9–16
Plugins (VST/AU)Parameters 1–8 of the plugin's listParameters 9–16

Knobs run left to right, top to bottom: the top-left knob is the first parameter. Turning one never makes the parameter jump, even after switching devices.

To choose a plugin's knobs: click the triangle on the plugin's title bar to unfold its parameters, click Configure, then touch the controls you want in the plugin's window, in the order you want them on the knobs. Click Configure again to finish. Live saves the list with the set.

If the knobs do nothing

Click the device you want so it has the blue hand. If they still do nothing, import the current preset3-keys.spc again with MidiSuite: an older one sends knob messages this version doesn't use.

Preset 2's PAD BANK

Drum pads

On the step sequencer's second pad bank, the pads play a Drum Rack directly, and they're velocity-sensitive: hit harder for louder. They don't light up, because the pads Live can light always send full velocity. The buttons and knobs stay on the sequencer's controls.

It needs MVave_SMC_KNOBS loaded, and the track's input on All Ins; see Setup. The notes arrive on MIDI channel 2.

Playing

  1. Put a Drum Rack on a MIDI track.
  2. Arm the track, or set its Monitor to In. Leave the track's input on All Ins and All Channels.
  3. Switch the controller to preset 2 with Shift + its pad, and press PAD BANK.
  4. Play. The pads match the Drum Rack's 16 pads as Live shows them: bottom-left is C1, usually the kick.

Recording a beat

Arm the track and click the record button in an empty clip slot, or use Live's session record. (On preset 1 an empty pad makes an empty 1-bar clip rather than recording: play it and turn on Session Record to record into it.) Or just play, then press Live's Capture MIDI button. Velocities go in as you played them.

To tidy the timing afterwards, select the notes in Live's clip view and press Cmd+U to quantise.

Once, before you start

Setup: which file goes where

Three things need to be in place: the presets on the controller, the scripts in Live's folder, and the ports in Live's settings. Paths below are inside the project folder unless they start with /.

These steps are for this Mac's setup. On Windows, or with Live's User Library somewhere else, follow INSTALL.md instead: it has the paths and port names for both, and how to check it worked.

1. Presets on the controller

Open MidiSuite, M-Vave's editor. Import each file and write it to its preset slot.

FilePreset slotGives you
presets/preset1-launcher-mixer.spcPreset 1Clip launcher; PAD BANK → mixer
presets/preset2-sequencer-drums.spcPreset 2Step sequencer; PAD BANK → drum pads
presets/preset3-keys.spcPreset 3Scale keyboard and device knobs; PAD BANK → key and scale

Only load files from presets. The reference folder holds the files the presets are built from (tools/make_presets.py rebuilds them) and the original editor exports.

2. Scripts in Live's folder

Copy these three folders into /Users/Shared/Ableton/User Library/Remote Scripts/, replacing any older copies, then restart Live:

FolderRuns
MVave_SMC_PADPads, buttons and lights for presets 1 and 3: clips, the mixer and the keyboard
MVave_SMC_KNOBSThe knobs on every preset, preset 2's drum pads and preset 3's note repeat
MVave_SMC_STEPSEQPreset 2's step sequencer

All three are needed: MVave_SMC_KNOBS also passes preset 2's drum pads and note repeat to your tracks, and runs preset 3's knobs.

3. Ports in Live

Open Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI. Fill three Control Surface rows:

Control SurfaceInputOutput
MVave_SMC_PADSINCO Port 3SINCO Port 3
MVave_SMC_KNOBSSINCO SMC-PAD-PrivateSINCO SMC-PAD-Private
MVave_SMC_STEPSEQSINCO Port 3SINCO Port 3

Then in the MIDI Ports list below the table, find the input SINCO SMC-PAD-Master, switch on Remote (so you can still map knobs with Cmd+M) and leave Track off. Leave it out of the Control Surface rows.

Preset 2's drum pads and note repeat reach your tracks through MVave_SMC_KNOBS, on MIDI channel 2. With Track on they'd play twice, and note repeat would ignore the keyboard's key and octave.

Quick fixes

If something's off

Pads do nothing

Check you're on the right preset for what you're doing. Remember PAD BANK flips the pads to a second layout: the mixer on preset 1, drum pads on preset 2, key and scale on preset 3. Drum pads and the keyboard only play into an armed track (or one set to Monitor In).

Pads stay dark in the launcher

Pads only light where there's a clip inside the coloured outline. Move the outline with the bottom two knobs on knob bank 2, or restart Live to reload the script.

Sequencer pads stay dark

Select a MIDI clip first. The sequencer edits whichever clip is selected, and with none selected it has nothing to show.

Drum pads or keyboard make no sound

Check the track is armed or set to Monitor In, with its input on All Ins, and that a synth set to one MIDI channel listens on channel 2 (or omni). Drum pads, note repeat and preset 3's knobs need MVave_SMC_KNOBS on input SINCO SMC-PAD-Private. The keyboard needs MVave_SMC_PAD in a Control Surface row on SINCO Port 3; if its pads stay dark, check that row and restart Live.

Empty pads show white

The preset on the controller doesn't match the scripts. In MidiSuite, M-Vave's editor, import the right file from the presets folder again: preset1-launcher-mixer.spc (preset 1), preset2-sequencer-drums.spc (preset 2) or preset3-keys.spc (preset 3).

Old lights stay after switching

The pads should redraw within about a fifth of a second of a preset switch or PAD BANK, or the moment you touch a pad. That needs MVave_SMC_KNOBS with both input and output on SINCO SMC-PAD-Private: it asks the controller which preset is up about five times a second. If they don't redraw, check that output isn't None, then the input, then restart Live.

The steady blinking of Live's MIDI light is those questions. It's a few hundred bytes a second, nothing the USB connection notices.

Pads launch the wrong clips

If every pad fires the clip three pads along and the last pads stay white, the controller holds a preset file from before 8 October 2026. Import all three files from presets again and save them to the controller.

Set up for one specific configuration: preset 1 preset1-launcher-mixer.spc, preset 2 preset2-sequencer-drums.spc, preset 3 preset3-keys.spc, scripts in /Users/Shared/Ableton/User Library/Remote Scripts/.
